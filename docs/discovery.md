## What people told us

### Small shops

| # | Who (anonymized) | What they said | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Metal fabricator, southwestern Ontario | "The issue is not certified welders… Smaller shops only get to look at painfully small jobs that have a lot of hoops… all admin, no shop time… The majority of us don't want to look at anything under $100,000 annually, or higher if it's a one-and-done job." | The pain is **job size and paperwork**, not only skilled workers. Shops want ongoing work that's worth the admin. |
| 2 | Precision machine shop, southwestern Ontario | "Our controlled goods certification has lapsed… As far as paperwork goes, the person that told you that is 100% correct." | Certificates lapse, and that quietly locks shops out. It's also a second confirmation that paperwork is the burden. |
| 3 | Plastics manufacturer, southwestern Ontario | "Quite a few quotes come through subcontractors that are less than minimum quantity… we quote the minimum and/or a setup charge (or decline). We have a few larger ongoing jobs… initially we had to go through a lot of paperwork but now it runs like any other project." | Shops need to **counter** with a minimum quantity or setup charge. Paperwork is a one-time hurdle, so do it once and reuse it. |
| 4 | Machine shop owner, southwestern Ontario | "Our scope would be more machinist than welders." | Training and matching must cover **every trade**, not just welding. |
| 5 | Electronics manufacturer, southwestern Ontario | "We don't do any welding for defense, but we design and build electronics subassemblies and box builds." | Match electronics and box-build work too. |
| 6 | Precision parts maker, southwestern Ontario | "Not a welding shop, this wouldn't apply to us." | Our first message read as welding-only. Fix the wording. |
| 7 | A sheet metal shop and a foundry, southwestern Ontario | Offered a call. | Warm leads. |

## What we changed in Shieldworks

| Feedback | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs too small, not worth the admin (1, 3) | **Right-sized work:** offers show yearly value and duration, a shop's jobs are grouped into one work package, shops can set a minimum, and offers below it are flagged |
| Minimum quantity and setup charges (3) | **Counter-offers:** a shop can accept with a setup charge or minimum quantity, and the defence contractor sees it and can respond |
| Why shops say no (1, 3, 6) | **New decline reasons** (too small, too much paperwork, below minimum quantity) and a "why shops said no" panel for the defence contractor |
| Paperwork is heavy but one-time (1, 2, 3) | **Paperwork once:** a shop keeps insurance, quality, NDA and Canadian-content papers on file once, and every award package reuses them |
| Lapsed certificates (2) | **Lapsed status and renewal:** expired certificates stop counting, the shop sees "Renew → N more jobs worth $X", and expiry alerts come early |
| Not only welders (4, 5, 6) | **Every trade:** training and matching for machinists, electronics and harness technicians, coating operators and inspectors; generic wording is trade-neutral |

## Open questions to take into calls

- What's the smallest yearly value that makes defence work worth it, and does bundling jobs change the answer?
- Which documents repeat for every contractor, and which differ?
- How often do certificates lapse, and what does renewal really take?
- For machinists and electronics technicians, which training would a contractor actually pay for?
